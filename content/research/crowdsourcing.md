Large-scale data management cannot rely on machines alone. Human judgment is essential for handling ambiguity, ensuring quality, and interpreting domain-specific context. My research develops **human-in-the-loop frameworks** that integrate crowd contributions intelligently at each stage of the data pipeline.

### Data collection and quality

I have developed models and tools for evaluating and improving data quality in crowdsourced settings, including crowd-based annotation tasks and quality metrics that account for worker expertise and bias {{< cite "bozzon2013reactive" >}} {{< cite "bozzon2015designing" >}}.

### Crowdsourcing platforms and patterns

Early work at Politecnico di Milano produced pattern-based specifications for crowdsourcing applications {{< cite "bozzon2014pattern" >}}, community-based crowdsourcing models {{< cite "brambilla2014community" >}}, and adaptive platform designs {{< cite "brambilla2015adaptive" >}}.

### Applications

Human-in-the-loop methods have been applied across several domains:

- **Music transcription** — microtask crowdsourcing for music score error detection {{< cite "samiotis2020microtask" >}}
- **Energy consumption** — user-generated content analysis for smart city energy behavior {{< cite "mauri2018social" >}} {{< cite "de2019automatic" >}}
- **Social research** — complementing studies on vulnerable youth with social media data {{< cite "mauri2021complementing" >}}
- **Privacy** — human-in-the-loop approaches to image privacy preservation {{< cite "mauri2021towards" >}}
